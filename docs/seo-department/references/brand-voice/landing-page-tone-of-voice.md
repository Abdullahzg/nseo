# ImagineArt Landing Page Tone of Voice

A working guide for hero lines, feature copy, CTAs, and microcopy across imagine.art.

## How to use this

This builds on the house voice already in use across ImagineArt content: direct, specific, human. Landing
pages get one more layer on top — a set of tones to pull from depending on the moment, plus a hard list of
words, punctuation, and sentence shapes to avoid because they read as generic AI copy. Nothing here replaces
judgment. If a line sounds like it could sit on any AI startup's homepage, cut it and try again.

## The tones

Pick a tone per section of a page, not per page. A hero can open confident, land one witty aside, and close
authoritative with a spec. What none of these tones can become is generic — that's the one failure mode
that isn't on this list because it isn't a tone at all.

**Authoritative** — Stripe is the reference point: its docs and landing pages read like the engineers who
built the product wrote them, not the people trying to sell it. That precision is what makes a claim like
"built for developers" credible instead of a slogan.
*"Grok Imagine renders native audio with every clip. No separate sound pass."* — not "Experience the future
of AI-powered content creation."

**Confident** — Apple built a design language around saying less and meaning more: "Shot on iPhone," not
"Capture stunning photography with our advanced camera system." Confidence is authority's louder, shorter
cousin.
*"One prompt. A finished shot."*

**Approachable** — Notion writes copy that assumes competence without talking down. Approachable means the
reader feels like they're talking to someone good at this, not a system.
*"No lighting rig, no location scout. Just an idea and a prompt box."*

**Witty — use sparingly** — Mailchimp's early marketing copy is the textbook case: jokes that never got in
the way of the point. On ImagineArt, wit is a wink, not a punchline — one line, then back to business.
*"Your camera crew called. They said take the day off."*
Where wit earns its place: hero one-liners, empty states, hover microcopy, loading messages, 404 pages,
changelog notes. Where it doesn't: pricing, comparison tables, legal and privacy copy, billing errors,
failed-generation errors, or anything with a dollar figure next to it. Rule of thumb: one joke per section,
maximum. If you have to explain the pun, cut it.

## Words we don't use

These have been used so often across AI marketing that they've stopped meaning anything. If a draft leans
on one of these, the actual idea usually isn't clear yet — go find the specific thing being said and say
that instead.

elevate, leverage, unlock, unleash, embark, delve, streamline, supercharge, harness the power of, empower,
ecosystem, synergy, holistic, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, world-class, best-in-class,
next-level, game-changing, game-changer, revolutionize, revolutionary, transformative, disruptive, paradigm
shift, frictionless, effortlessly, plethora, myriad, boundless, endless possibilities, tapestry, realm,
landscape (as metaphor), testament to, unparalleled, unrivaled, meticulously, curated, bespoke (unless
literally custom-made), navigate the complexities of, at the intersection of, in today's fast-paced world,
ever-evolving landscape, when it comes to, in order to, utilize, actionable insights, pain points,
stakeholders, solutions (as filler noun), in conclusion, it's important to note, whether or not, the future
is here.

Also retired: generic taglines like "Welcome to the future of creativity," "Bring your vision to life,"
"Powered by advanced AI," and "Trusted by thousands of creators worldwide" with no name or number attached.

## Punctuation we avoid

- **Em dash** — off the table. It's the single most reliable tell of AI-generated copy. Restructure into
  two sentences instead.
- More than one exclamation mark per page.
- Scare quotes for emphasis (the "smart" way to create).
- Title Case Headlines For Every Word — sentence case only.
- Semicolons in headlines or CTAs (fine, rarely, in body copy).
- Ellipses used as a suspense device ("Something amazing is coming…").

## Sentence shapes that read as generic AI copy

These patterns are individually harmless and collectively a tell. If more than one shows up on a page, a
model probably wrote the draft.

**"It's not just X, it's Y"**
Don't: *This isn't just an image generator, it's a creative partner.*
Do: *Type a prompt. Get a shot you'd actually use.*

**"Whether you're a X or a Y" opener**
Don't: *Whether you're a solo creator or a full production team, ImagineArt has you covered.*
Do: *A solo creator can generate a full ad set before a production team finishes casting.*

**Tricolon padding**
Don't: *Fast. Powerful. Seamless.*
Do: *Under 30 seconds per render, at up to 4K.*

**Rhetorical question + stock answer**
Don't: *Ever wondered how top brands create scroll-stopping content? Here's how.*
Do: *Most scroll-stopping ads are shot in an afternoon now, not a week.*

**Participial throat-clearing opener**
Don't: *Designed to empower creators everywhere, ImagineArt is built to help you tell your story.*
Do: *ImagineArt turns a text prompt into a finished video.*

**Hedge stacking**
Don't: *This could potentially help you create content more efficiently.*
Do: *This cuts a two-week shoot down to an afternoon.*

**Filler sign-offs**
Don't: *The possibilities are endless. Let's dive in.*
Do: *Try it on your next brief. First video's free.*

## Before / after

**Hero**
Don't: *Unlock the future of content creation. Our cutting-edge AI platform empowers creators to seamlessly
transform their vision into stunning, professional-grade visuals — whether you're a solo creator or an
enterprise team.*
Do: *Type a prompt. Get a shot a studio would sign off on. No crew, no rig, no shot list.*

**Feature copy**
Don't: *Leverage our robust suite of AI-powered tools to elevate your fashion photography workflow to the
next level.*
Do: *Photograph one outfit. Generate the model, the pose, and the studio light for every angle after.*

**CTA**
Don't: *Ready to embark on your creative journey? Sign up today and experience the future!*
Do: *Try it on your next brief. First video's free.*

## Before you ship a page

- Read it aloud — if it sounds like a pitch deck, rewrite it.
- No word from the banned list, anywhere, including alt text and meta descriptions.
- No em dashes.
- At most one exclamation mark on the page.
- At most one joke per section, and it lands without an explanation.
- Every claim has a name, a number, or a spec behind it — not an adjective.
- Sentence case in every headline.
- If it could sit on any other AI startup's homepage, it's not done yet.
